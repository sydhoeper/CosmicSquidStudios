Chambers of Becoming

A walkable tunnel system where a fictional civilization practices emotional honesty without shame.

ContextSchool project
PromptStructures

FormatUnity walkthrough
Emotions are signals, not moral failures.

Emora believed suffering came less from emotion itself than from shame and repression.

Slide 1 of 7

The concept

Safe, communal chambers for confronting desire, grief, rage, envy, and fear—then deciding how to respond.

Belief Emotion is information

Feelings signal needs, boundaries, attachments, and change.

Practice Architecture supports reflection

Visitors physically enter and sit with an emotion.

Atmosphere Safe, strange, sacred

Organic forms and filtered light make introspection feel protected.

How the chambers connect

A circular room opens into five emotional chambers. Only Desire was developed for this assignment.

The Desire Chamber

The Devil tarot card became a framework for exploring pleasure, compulsion, attachment, and agency.

Core question

Pleasure or compulsion?

The chamber does not tell visitors what they should want. It asks them to be honest about why they want it—and whether desire supports their life or controls it.

Symbol Loose chains preserve agency

The figures appear bound, but what restrains them can be removed.

Guardian Face the shadow self

A devil figure guards honesty about appetite, sexuality, and control.

Space Make room to sit with desire

Cushioned alcoves, symbolic objects, and low light turn the chamber into a place for private reflection.

The build

I sculpted the tunnels at human scale in VR, surfaced them in Substance Painter, assembled the scene in Unity, and built a first-person route through it.

Adobe MediumVR sculpting → Substance PainterSurface and texture → UnityScene assembly → Asset mixFree + Meshy AI → POV buildWalkable experience

Result

A philosophical concept became a place I could walk through.

The final Unity scene gave the original proposal spatial rhythm, material detail, and a clear point of view. It remains one of the school projects I am most proud of.
