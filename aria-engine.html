ARIA Engine

As a technical artist, I populated the 200+ existing rooms in artist Peter Burr’s underground skyscraper with story-driven details, then framed them as cinematic interstitials.

My roleTechnical artist
Scale200+ rooms populated
ToolsUnity, prefabs, Meshy AI
StatusMulti-year artwork in progress

Slide 1 of 6

The installation scans a visitor’s face, generates a character, and sends that character walking through the “dirtscraper” on the large screen.

The assignment

Give every room a distinct past while staying inside Peter’s visual system and the project’s technical constraints.

Direction

One prompt per room

Peter’s AI prompts supplied the premise; I translated each one into a physical scene.

Constraint

A limited prefab kit

Repeated objects had to feel fresh across hundreds of spaces.

Goal

Make people wonder

Every room needed enough evidence to provoke: “What happened here?”

How I populated the world

Working closely with Peter in Missoula, I used a three-part process to give the existing rooms story and movement.

  1. Interpret the prompt

    Find the room’s story. Peter’s prompt guided who had occupied the space and what might have happened there.

  2. Stage the evidence

    Arrange, reuse, and review. I recombined limited prefabs into knocked-over, sprawling scenes, used the Meshy workflow when needed, and checked the results with Peter.

  3. Compose the interstitials

    Turn rooms into moving images. Once the rooms were populated, I placed two cameras in each and composed slow clips for the installation slideshow.

What I delivered

A populated world with enough specificity to support both active character exploration and the quiet interstitial moments between visitors.

Worldbuilding

200+ populated rooms

Each space carries its own premise, aftermath, and visual clues.

Cinematography

Two views per room

Camera compositions turned the completed environments into a library of slow interstitial clips.

Collaboration

One cohesive vision

Close daily review balanced my creative contribution with Peter’s authorship and direction.
